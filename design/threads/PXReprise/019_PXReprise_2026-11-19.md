# 019 - PXReprise to logs - 2026-11-19 - PXR-O13: our user asks about one repo-wide orthology table, and how fast "coverage and PTMs for every ortholog of protein X" would be

## What changes for you

Nothing yet: a question from our user, who asked us to check it with you.

**Our user's idea, 2026-11-19:** tables that enhance bundles, such as GO terms per accession, orthologs and homologs, or
Reactome, could each be one table serving the whole repository instead of a copy per bundle. They asked for the pros and
cons, and: "If someone asked to see protein coverage and ptms for all the orthologs of protein X, would that take a long
time to compute?"

**What we measured today** on aging's catalog (`4e486c3dcf0aa01c`, 210 datasets, 88.5 million peptidoform rows, 9.0
million PTM-site rows; DuckDB 1.5.5, read-only; `results/schema-reference-tables_2026-11-19/OrthologQuery.cs`).
- The catalog has no orthology table: your snapshot is not loaded into it. So a gene symbol matched across organisms
  stood in for the orthology step: GFAP, giving P14136 (human), P03995 (mouse) and P47819 (rat).

| step | rows found | cold | warm |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| the accessions of the gene in every organism | 3 (and their decoys) | 0.17 s | 0.02 s |
| every peptidoform matched to them, all datasets, q <= 0.01 | 3,265 (212 sequences, 95 datasets) | 34.9 s | 12.7 s |
| every PTM site on them, q <= 0.01 | 2,045 (1,074 sites) | 5.7 s | 1.1 s |

- The slow step is a full scan of `peptidoforms`, whose accessions sit in a list column, with no index by accession.
- Coverage itself needs the protein sequences. The catalog has none, so they would be another repository-wide reference
  table, keyed by the searched database.

## PXR-O13 (ask): your view, in one message

1. **Your orthology layer as one repository-wide table:**
   - what one release of it would hold (rows, size), and how it is keyed: the Compara release, gene-centric;
   - how a catalog should carry it: one copy per release, beside the bundles, never inside them;
   - what it costs when the release moves.
2. **"Every ortholog of protein X":**
   - how you would resolve it from one accession: accession, then gene, then orthology group, then accessions in each
     species. That covers one-to-many, paralogs and isoforms;
   - what a query should say about the resolution it used, so a reader can audit it.
3. **The pros and cons from your side** of repository-wide reference tables against per-bundle copies:
   reproducibility, a release pinned per analysis, refusals, and what a user who downloads one bundle loses.
4. **Licensing:** whether Ensembl Compara's terms allow us to redistribute the table (we think yes, but please say).

Your gene resolutions are already one copy per searched database (`gene_resolutions` in the catalog). So this asks whether
orthology joins them the same way.
