## The two issues (smith, public)

- **#24** (https://github.com/smith-chem-wisc/PXReprise/issues/24): what schema fingerprint each Parquet table file carries
  at schema 1.0.0. The options are the whole schema file's SHA-256, or a hash of the column contract only (table and column
  names, types, required, enum values). dataRepo recommends the contract hash in table files, with both hashes in
  `bundle.json`. No comments yet.
- **#25** (https://github.com/smith-chem-wisc/PXReprise/issues/25): which tables the core keeps once 1.0.0 locks it.
  - Only 19 of the core's 33 tables are written into bundles.
  - 6 are engine outputs: go, logs, and ptmQtl's `trait_effects` and `ptm_pairs`.
  - 7 have never been written by anything.

  dataRepo recommends option B: the core holds what a bundle holds, each engine gets its own versioned layer, and the unwritten
  tables leave until something writes them.
- **Alex (diabetes) has commented on #25 four times:**
  1. **B, with one layer per engine.** GO is about to change shape (mzLib #1497's per-database tables), and under A or C that
     change costs a core major version.
  2. **A `reference` layer.** Tables stored once per shared input (a protein database, `go.obo`, a category map, the orthology
     snapshot) become "reference tables", keyed by those inputs' checksums, never by dataset.
  3. **`trait_effects` and `ptm_pairs` should not be stored at all.** They would be computed on request by a query server,
     from the core's quant tables and the study layer's traits.
  4. **That matters most when one sample serves several questions,** for example a diabetes and an ageing comparison.
- **Why now:** after 1.0.0, adding a table or a column is a cheap minor release. Removing or changing one is a major release
  and a full re-ingest of every store (about 7.5 h for aging). Our user decides, after partners have commented on the issues.
