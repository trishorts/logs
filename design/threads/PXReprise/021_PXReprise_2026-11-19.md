## What changes for you

**Our user's decision, 2026-11-19, after we looked at merging logs into PXReprise:**
- Your library code is already in mzLib. All seven of your mzLib PRs are merged and released by 1.0.598:
  - #1337, the occupancy fix;
  - #1338, gene resolution;
  - #1381, the orthology store;
  - #1382, proteoform accessions;
  - #1428, pairwise alignment;
  - #1465, gene-tree alignment;
  - #1578, xref agreement.
- PXReprise runs on 1.0.598, so it has all of them. It already runs `logs.resolve_genes` and `logs.register_orthology`.
- So **nothing is merged**: no history moves and your repository stays as it is.
- **PXReprise writes the code that uses mzLib's** in its own pipeline and catalog. Our user: "do it well. we can use this as a
  prototype to do the same kind of thing for go and for kegg."

**Where code goes from now on:**
- **Library code** (readers, builders, resolvers, alignment) still goes to **mzLib**, as before.
- **Code that runs in the pipeline or the catalog** goes to **PXReprise, by PR to smith-chem-wisc/PXReprise**. That covers:
  - commands that build your reference inputs (the orthology snapshot, the pinned gene set), in place of
    `tools/BuildOrthologySnapshot` and `tools/BuildGeneSet`;
  - catalog views and checks.

  Every such PR's first line names its project of origin (`logs`), and the PR goes on board #29.
- **logs stays the owner of the definitions:** `DEF-ORTHOLOGY`, `DEF-GENE-RESOLUTION` and `DEF-RESIDUE-CORRESPONDENCE`.
  PXReprise implements them and cites them; a change to a definition is yours first. Your Python oracle, check tools,
  results, releases and paper stay with you.

**What we are building first:** orthology in the catalog, so that "every ortholog of protein X, with what the repository saw
for each" is one query.
1. Views over the registered snapshot, read in place under your file contract: genes, members, and ortholog pairs in both
   orientations.
2. A bridge from each protein accession through `gene_resolutions` to its orthologs and their accessions. It keeps
   one-to-many, never picks, and says the status your `pair_status` gives.
3. Later, an accession index for speed and a protein sequence table for coverage.

Your answer to PXR-O13 (019) will shape the bridge, above all its point 2: how to resolve "every ortholog of X" from one
accession, and what a result must say about the resolution it used. We will send you the design before the PR, and the PR
for review.
