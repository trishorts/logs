## 1 - The compare view

- **`orthologs(root, a, b)`**: one row per ortholog between species `a` and `b`, with `a`'s gene first.
  One-to-many is kept as several rows and never collapsed. Each row carries `relationship_type`,
  `is_high_confidence` (null when Compara gives none), `goc_score`, `wga_coverage` and both identity
  percentages, all as Compara states them.
- **`pair_status(root, a, b)`**: every gene of `a`, each with exactly one reason:
  `has_ortholog`, `no_edge_in_shared_tree`, `tree_lacks_target_species` or `not_in_any_tree`. This is
  the "typed reason when there is none". It carries `gene_biotype`, so you choose the denominator and
  name it.
- **`species_set3(root, a, b, c)`**: human/mouse/rat tuples that are orthologs in **all three** pairs,
  flagged `all_one2one`. There are 15,508 human genes in such a one-to-one tuple, the figure our 007
  gave dataRepo, now read back from the snapshot.

```sql

-- rat genes with their human orthologs; join your gene rows on gene
SELECT * FROM orthologs('E:/CodeReview/logs/snapshots/compara-116', 'rattus_norvegicus', 'homo_sapiens');
```

**Two things to carry into any rat-vs-human result:**
- **Join from your default gene view** (`ensembl_xref_agrees = true`), and say so. The rat gene
  coverage from 013 (59.6% of entries) comes before orthology. An entry with no gene has no ortholog
  row, and it is not a `pair_status` class either.
- **Check the gene set first.** `manifest.json`'s `gene_set_sha256` must equal the
  `gene_set_sha256` on your resolution rows. For the human, mouse and rat 116 resolutions you already
  have, it does.
