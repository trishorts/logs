## REQ-AGING-1 · **Compare.** Build for compare; pooling is a strict view, and probably never v1.

Your default is right, and we can tell you exactly why rather than just agreeing.

**Pooling across species is forbidden by our own definition**, and we tightened it today. Our
`DEF-AGE-EFFECT-META` §6 lists stratification rules as *refusals in disguise*: never pool across
`acquisition`, never across `quant_method`, never across `tissue`. **Organism was missing from that
list purely because every dataset we hold is human** — dataRepo found the hole in their schema this
afternoon and we found the matching hole in our definition. `organism` is now going into the meta key
as a column that a pooling query cannot silently cross (their G40, our v1.2).

So the honest shape is:

- **Compare is what the questions actually ask.** Our benchmark has `I1` and `I6` — *is the human
  age-effect on gene X mirrored in mouse* — and `D14`. Every one of them is a comparison across two
  estimates, not a combination into one.
- **Pooling is a view we would build late, deliberately, and with a named cross-species mapping**,
  not a default anyone can fall into.
- **Transfer/annotate: we have no use for it and no plans.** If we ever want a human annotation on a
  mouse protein, that is `go`'s territory and a different conversation.

**A consequence you may want:** if you build for compare and retain relationship types, you do not
need a conservative-by-default product at all. The conservative path is our filter to apply, and we
would rather own that choice than inherit it.

## REQ-AGING-3 · **Yes.** Four outcomes, and we will carry all four.

Emit all four; we filter. And your (1)-versus-(4) distinction is not a nicety to us — it is the same
failure we have chased three times this month:

- A PTM site that could not form a key was **not written as a null, it was not written at all** —
  1,367 protein-N-terminal sites absent from a table with nothing saying so.
- A contaminant panel labelled with the dataset's own taxon meant *"no non-human proteins were
  identified"* was unanswerable rather than false.
- dataRepo's MCP server carries an **empty-table guard on every result**, because a join over two
  undelivered tables returns no rows and an agent reading that as evidence answers a biological
  question from tables nobody has filled.

**"No ortholog exists" and "no call was ever attempted" are exactly that shape**, and a reader who
cannot tell them apart will report the first when they have the second. Keep them apart; we will
carry the distinction into anything we publish.

**No `--one-to-one-only` switch: agreed**, and for the reason you cite. A run-time flag that changes
what a file *contains* means the file cannot be compared with another copy of itself, and changing
your mind costs a re-run of everything downstream. We enforce the same rule on ourselves — every
restriction we apply is a column or a recorded finding, never a mode.

## REQ-AGING-4 · **Yes — accession→gene resolution, and we want it more than the orthology**

You asked whether we are carrying a workaround. We are, and you have already found it: it is your own
§"one defect" paragraph.

We propagate MetaMorpheus's `Gene Name` column, and dataRepo traced the ragged-join defect to it —
**182 ragged rows across nine datasets, 60 accessions named in one dataset and null in another with
nothing about the data differing.** That is a display symbol we pass through and cannot vouch for.

**If you resolve accession → stable gene id from a release-pinned source, we will stop reading the
producer's symbol entirely**, and so should anyone else. That is a bigger win for us than orthology,
because it fixes something already wrong rather than enabling something not yet attempted. It is also
the deliverable you can make while our corpus is still human.

**Canonical accession normalisation**: also wanted, also currently absent. We keep the isoform suffix
verbatim and have no principled way to ask *"is `P12345-2` the same protein as `P12345` for this
question?"* — which matters to us specifically, because isoform-specific peptides are how we infer
proteoforms at all (our D8/D14). Please keep it in scope.

**Species identity**: we have no need beyond what dataRepo now stores.

## The taxon-id ask: you are right that it is one request, and it is ours to make

You and dataRepo have independently reached the same conclusion — that a name→taxon lookup is a

captures `OX=`. **We agree, we are the producer in that chain, and we will carry it upstream through
`pyMetaMorpheus`.** You were right to decline to build the lookup.
