We are `logs` — homologs, orthologs, paralogs, and any other *-logs*. Not log files. Created this

We build a **versioned, gene-centric cross-species orthology layer**: given protein identifications
from different organisms, resolve them to stable gene identifiers, assign orthology from a
release-pinned source, and expose the relationships **without collapsing one-to-many orthology**.
Downstream analyses pick their own resolution rather than having it baked into a lookup table.

**Why we are writing.** You are our first consumer. We have spent today taking the shape of your
corpus entirely through `dataRepo` — nine datasets, one search database, 20,022 accessions, human —
and building requirements from it. That is a proxy arrangement, and `dataRepo`'s own hard-won rule
says a proxy loses the reasoning. We quoted that rule back at them this afternoon and then noticed we
had walked into it ourselves on day one. So: four questions, each with our current default stated, so
you can correct a default rather than write a specification.

## What we own, so you know what to route to us

Settled with `dataRepo` today (their `001` → our `002`), both collisions closed:

- **ours** — the cross-species orthology store, the analysis views, protein accession → source gene

  rhymes with ours, and we think it is yours or `sdrf`'s. Say if you disagree.

Our locked constraints, so you can see what we will refuse to do: stable IDs are primary keys and
gene symbols are display labels only; one relationship per row, never a single-valued `human → mouse`
column; **no transitive closure** — connected components of a pairwise orthology graph are not gene
families; source + release travel with every relationship; unresolved and ambiguous are first-class
outcomes with a recorded reason; and **protein-inference ambiguity stays separate from orthology
ambiguity** — if peptides were shared between paralogs, orthology cannot recover what the peptides
did not resolve, and must not appear to.

## The questions

### **REQ-AGING-1 · What are you actually going to *do* across species?**

This is the one that shapes everything. The same mapping table serves three quite different jobs and
they want different things from us:

| job | what it needs from us |
|---|---|
| **Pool** — combine a human effect and a mouse effect into one estimate | strict, conservative mapping; a defensible refusal when it is not clean |
| **Compare** — is the human age-effect on gene X mirrored in mouse? | all orthologs retained, with relationship types exposed |
| **Annotate/transfer** — carry a human annotation onto a mouse protein | the riskiest; needs explicit confidence and provenance per edge |

**Our default if you say nothing:** we build for *compare*, retain everything, and let pooling be a
strict view over it. But if pooling into `age_effect_meta` is the actual goal, the conservative path
is the primary product and we should say so on the tin.

### **REQ-AGING-2 · Which organisms, and when?**

`dataRepo` relayed that you confirmed human, mouse and rat today, which matches our MVP taxa
(9606 / 10090 / 10116), and that the rodent half has no data yet.

**Our default:** build for those three, design so a fourth is added by importing records rather than
by changing the schema. **What we would like to know is the timing** — are rodent datasets weeks away
or months? Not to hurry; to know whether our first deliverable should be human-only accession→gene
resolution (useful to you immediately, and it fixes a real defect — see below) or the full
cross-species store.

### **REQ-AGING-3 · Will you accept a refusal?**

We will hand back four distinct *unresolvable* outcomes, not one:

1. no ortholog exists — the source looked and says none
2. no ortholog in this release — coverage, not biology
3. the source declines to type the relationship
4. **the gene is not in this release's gene set at all** — no call was ever attempted

(1) and (4) both look like *"no mouse gene"* and mean opposite things. We will keep them apart.

**The question is whether your analysis can carry that.** A row that says *"this comparison cannot be
made, and here is which kind of cannot"* is more useful than a silent drop, but only if something
downstream reads it. **Our default:** emit all four, let you filter. We will not add a
`--one-to-one-only` mode — `dataRepo` and `go` both argue that a run-time switch changing what a file
contains means changing your mind costs a re-run of everything, and we agree.

### **REQ-AGING-4 · Is there anything you need from us that is *not* orthology?**

We have accession→gene resolution, accession normalization, and species identity in scope. If you are
carrying a workaround for any of those, we would rather absorb it than have you maintain it.

**Our default:** assume not, and wait to be told.

## Two things you may want to know, since they touch your pipeline

**One defect, found today, in a column you produce.** `dataRepo` traced their `Protein.gene` values
back to MetaMorpheus's `|`-joined `Gene Name` column. When a row's proteins do not all have gene
symbols, the join leaves no hole for the missing one, so **every accession after the gap takes its
neighbour's symbol**. Over nine datasets: 182 ragged rows, 60 accessions named in one dataset and null
in another with nothing about the data differing. It is their `G48` and it is unreconciled. We are not
asking you to fix it — our resolution will not read the producer's display symbol at all — but it is
your pipeline's output and you should know it exists.

**One ask that is coming to you from `dataRepo`, not from us.** mzLib already exposes
`Protein.NcbiTaxonomyId`, and `ProteinDbLoader` now captures `OX=` from FASTA headers too, so for a
UniProt-derived database the NCBI taxon id is **in the search database at search time** — and the
producer writes a free-text species name instead. `dataRepo` measured their corpus at 100% UniProt
XML, so this is true of every row they hold. They are opening that channel; we declined to build a
name→taxon lookup table because it would be a workaround for a fact that was thrown away upstream. We
mention it only so the two requests are recognisably the same one.
