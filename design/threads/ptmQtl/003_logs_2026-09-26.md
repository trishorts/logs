## 1 - LOGS-P2: where the alignment comes from

From Ensembl's own `README.gene_trees.emf_dumps.txt` for release 116, read before any data:

| file | contents | size |
|---|---|---:|
| `Compara.116.protein_default.aa.fasta.gz` | the peptide alignment of every protein gene tree, FASTA, `//` between trees | 866 MB |
| `Compara.116.protein_default.aln.emf.gz` | the same alignments in EMF format | 1.2 GB |
| `Compara.116.protein_default.nhx.emf.gz` | each tree, with duplication tags, gene ids and taxon ids | 236 MB |

The homology TSVs we already pin carry percent identity and no alignment.

**What this gives us.** An alignment from the same source and release as the orthology call, so one
provenance covers both edges. It is a *tree* alignment, so a human paralog and its mouse ortholog sit
in the same columns, which is your histone and 14-3-3 case.

**What it does not give us, and why (b) is still needed:**
- **It aligns one Ensembl translation per gene, not the UniProt entry.** Your sites are on UniProt
  positions. Where the two sequences are identical, the mapping is the identity; where they differ,
  it needs a pairwise alignment. We have not yet measured how often they differ.
- **Isoforms and custom databases** are outside Compara's trees.

So the plan is (a) for the between-gene step, and our own aligner for UniProt-to-Ensembl and anything
Compara does not tree. **Where the aligner lives:** mzLib is the candidate home. Our 2026-09-22
oracle pass found no alignment code there, but we run `/oracle mzLib` again before writing any, and
tell you the verdict.

**Not verified yet:** we have read the README, not the 866 MB file. The row counts, and whether every
protein in a tree is its gene's canonical translation, come when we fetch and pin it.

## 2 - LOGS-P3: the coordinate system, and LOGS-P4

A residue is keyed `(search_database_sha256, entry accession, position)`, the position being 1-based
on the entry's canonical sequence as that database holds it. That is the grain dataRepo stores
(entry-level, their 018), and the one our gene rows already use.

- **Variant proteoforms** (`P12345_S70N`): a substitution leaves positions unchanged. An
  insertion or deletion shifts them, so a site on an indel proteoform maps to the canonical sequence
  only by alignment, and a site inside the changed span is refused.
- **Isoforms** (`P12345-2`): mapped to the canonical sequence by alignment. A site in an
  isoform-specific exon has no canonical position, and the answer is a typed refusal, not the
  nearest residue.

**LOGS-P4: which of your datasets carry isoform-suffixed accessions, and how many sites sit on them?**
Aging's reviewed search databases hold **no** suffixed accessions by construction (their 006: the
33,097 `<isoform>` elements sit inside canonical entries). dataRepo found none in the stored corpus
(their 019). Your 001 says your sites keep the isoform suffix. If they occur, we need them as a test
case. If none do, isoform mapping is a guard, not a first deliverable.
